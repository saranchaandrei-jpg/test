## 1. PECR email: corporate vs sole trader/partnership [SEARCH]
- Corporate subscribers (companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships, government bodies): the PECR consent rule for email/text does not apply, so you can email them without prior consent. You must still identify yourself and give a valid opt-out address. ICO says you should honour opt-outs even though PECR does not strictly require it.
- Sole traders, English/Welsh/NI non-LLP partnerships, other unincorporated bodies: treated as individuals. Need prior consent or the soft opt-in (contact details obtained in a sale or negotiation of a sale, similar products, opt-out at collection and in every message).
- You cannot tell status from the email address. If unsure, ICO advises treating the person as an individual subscriber.
- Implication for a first contact with no prior sale: for individuals there is no soft opt-in, so you need consent. Do not email unverified small-business contacts unless you have checked their status (e.g. Companies House).

## 2. Corporate address vs named individual at Ltd
- PECR: the corporate/individual test is about the subscriber (the Ltd), so name@ltd.co.uk and info@ltd.co.uk are both PECR-outside-consent. [SEARCH, consistent with ICO B2B page; wording not read.]
- UK GDPR still applies if the address identifies a living person (john.smith@...). Generic role addresses (info@, sales@) are generally not personal data, but this is not verified here. [BACKGROUND] Check the B2B page.

## 3. UK GDPR [BACKGROUND unless noted; read the lawful basis page above]
- Lawful basis: legitimate interests is usually used for B2B email where PECR does not require consent. Document a three-part LIA: purpose, necessity, balancing test. Consent is required instead where PECR requires it (individual subscribers).
- Transparency: Art.14 applies when you get data from a source other than the person (e.g. scraped or bought lists). Notice is due within a reasonable period, at most one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Privacy notice link in the first email is the usual approach. Exemptions exist (e.g. disproportionate effort) but the ICO is strict on them for marketing.
- Right to object to direct marketing is absolute (Art.21). Tell people of it at first contact; keep a suppression list (the minimum data needed to avoid re-contacting) rather than deleting.
- Source/provenance: record where each address came from.

## 4. Social media DMs to personal profiles [SEARCH]
- ICO lists direct messages via social media (and similar stored messages) as "electronic mail" under PECR. Same corporate/individual split applies.
- A personal Facebook profile is an individual: you would need consent (soft opt-in unlikely to apply to a cold first contact). So cold DMs to individuals' personal profiles are high risk. A DM to an identifiable Ltd's business page is arguably a corporate subscriber, but ICO view not verified.
- Also check platform terms (Meta anti-spam/automation rules) which are separate from the law.

## 5. Telephone / TPS / CTPS [BACKGROUND, not fetched]
- Live calls: must not call numbers on TPS (individuals/sole traders) or CTPS (corporate subscribers: companies, LLPs, etc.) unless the person has told you they do not object. Screen against both. Must give caller identity and a number; automated calls need consent.
- Verify current rules on the ICO "telephone marketing" pages before use.

## 6. Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 [SEARCH, secondary sources]
- PECR fines aligned with UK GDPR: up to GBP 17.5m or 4% of worldwide turnover, whichever higher (previously GBP 500k). Sources: Blake Morgan and MFMAC commentary (law firms), not primary text.
  - https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/data-use-and-access-act-2025-privacy-and-electronic-communications-regulations/
  - https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-increased-maximum-fines-for-cookies-and-direct-marketing-practices/
- Charity soft opt-in (new reg 22(3A)): ICO page said it expected commencement January 2026; one secondary source says effective 5 February 2026. UNCERTAIN, verify. Not relevant to a commercial start-up.
- Other: cookie rules extended to instigators; ICO said DUAA changes phased June 2025 to June 2026. By Oct 2026 the PECR/fines changes are likely in force, but I could not confirm commencement regulations. Check legislation.gov.uk and ICO's DUAA hub.
- The Act does not appear to remove the PECR consent rule for individuals or create a general B2C soft opt-in extension (from search summaries; verify).
